Matters of Method

This is the model from the course notebook 20_JulesVerneBot_Generating_English_Texts_with_RNN.ipynb, served directly to the browser. It is deliberately small and transparent:

  • Character-level tokenization. Every character in the ten novels is a token, giving a vocabulary of 123 symbols — letters, punctuation, accents and the odd Greek delta.
  • Two architectures, one tokenizer. The selector above switches between the character-level GRU from the course notebook and a decoder-only Transformer trained on the identical 123-character vocabulary. Both are size-matched at about 4 million parameters, so a difference in the writing is a difference in architecture, not in scale. A 25M-parameter Transformer is included for the scale comparison.
  • Temperature. Logits are divided by T, then softmaxed and sampled. As T → 0 the model becomes deterministic; as T grows the output grows stranger.
  • Sampling. Top-k masks all but the k likeliest characters. Greedy decoding is the T → 0 limit and tends to loop.
  • Cost. The RNN carries a hidden state, so each character costs one fixed step (~0.15 ms). The Transformer carries a KV cache and costs ~3.5 ms per character at 4M parameters, or ~29 ms at 25M — the O(C) attention cost made visible. Both are flat per character: the cost does not grow with how long the text has already become.

Inference here is pure NumPy — the trained weights are read straight from the .keras archive and the GRU recurrence is reimplemented, so the server needs no TensorFlow and answers in milliseconds. That also exposes the arithmetic for teaching.

Known limitations: a 120-character window and no attention mean the prose holds local grammar but loses the thread of a long narrative — the same point the notebook makes when bridging to modern transformer LLMs.
